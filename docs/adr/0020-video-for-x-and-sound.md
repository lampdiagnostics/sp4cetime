# ADR-0020: Videos made for X, sound, and the ground under a Starsigns place

Status: accepted (4 November 2026, 0.4.2).

## Context

The owner posted a one-minute Starsigns video (graphics only, MP4, 15.6 MB) to X on a new Premium account, and X rejected it. Length and size were far inside X's limits (140 s and 512 MB even for free accounts), so the account was not the cause. X is strict about what is inside an MP4 (the owner checked with Grok, whose advice matches X's published media rules):

1. **Video must be H.264** (not HEVC, VP9 …), **sound AAC-LC** (not MP3, Opus, HE-AAC).
2. **A sound track should be there.** Graphics exported without one are often refused; a silent AAC track fixes it.
3. **Constant frame rate**, ideally 30 frames a second. Screen and canvas recordings are usually variable.
4. **4:2:0 pixels (yuv420p), even width and height, at most 60 frames a second, aspect between 1:2.39 and 2.39:1**, progressive, closed groups of pictures.
5. **The index (moov) at the front** ("web optimised", `+faststart`).

Our recordings came from `MediaRecorder`, which stamps frames with wall-clock time (variable rate), gives no sound track for a canvas, and (in Chrome) puts the index at the end. The "Small, for X" size (0.4.0: 720p at about 2 Mbit/s, about 15 MB a minute, which matches the rejected file) also smeared fine lines. The owner also asked for a little more resolution and brightness in recordings, for GIFs whose thin lines (constellations, spirals) survive, for sound in the Starsigns path (silence in space; crickets and distant traffic on the ground), for a landing in a grassy field, and for each body to be looked at once.

## Decision

1. **Encode with WebCodecs, pack with our own MP4 writer.** `src/app/videoEncode.ts` feeds the recording canvas to the browser's `VideoEncoder` (H.264 High, level 4.0 for 1080p30, 5.1 for 4K; Main or Baseline only if High is refused) and the sound to `AudioEncoder` (AAC-LC, 48 kHz stereo, 160 kbit/s). `src/core/mp4.ts` (pure, tested in Node, no dependency, like `pdf.ts`) writes ftyp, moov, then mdat, samples interleaved by time, one sample per chunk, 32-bit offsets (files under 4 GB). A library (mp4-muxer) was considered: MIT but deprecated, and its successor (Mediabunny) is MPL-2.0, outside our licence rule; the writer we need is ~250 lines.
2. **Constant 30 frames a second by construction.** Frame *n* is stamped *n*/30 s. Each screen frame fills the video slots that have passed since the last one: a slow frame is encoded again (up to a second's worth), never skipped, so the video's clock matches real time and the sound stays in step. Keyframe every 2 s; no B-frames are asked for (the writer handles reordered frames with a `ctts` table if an encoder makes them anyway).
3. **A sound track always.** What is heard on the page (`src/app/sound.ts`) is tapped and encoded; any stretch without sound is encoded silence. Where the browser has no AAC encoder (Firefox, Linux Chromium), the writer fills the track with a constant silent AAC-LC frame (`SILENT_AAC_STEREO`, six bytes, checked to decode to −91 dB).
4. **Size within X's limits:** the Video size choice (Capture menu and the Starsigns dialog) keeps its two settings: **For X** (was "Small, for X", 720p) is now the screen's shape scaled to fit 1920 × 1080 (or 1080 × 1920 upright), even on both sides, about 0.2 bits per pixel per frame (12 Mbit/s at 1080p30; X allows 25); **Hi-res** fits 3840 × 2160 for desktop screens and is not for X. Both use the same encoder, so both have constant frame rate and sound.
5. **Resolution held at the video's own.** While recording, the views are drawn at the resolution the video needs (`holdQuality(true, scale)` in `src/core/quality.ts`, up to 3 device px per CSS px), not at whatever lower resolution the frame-rate governor had dropped to; afterwards the governor returns to the screen's own.
6. **A little pop.** Videos and GIFs draw the views through a canvas filter: brightness ×1.18, contrast ×1.08, saturation ×1.15 (in the Bright look: contrast and saturation only, so the ink darkens). Names, boxes and the watermark are not filtered.
7. **GIFs:** each view is thickened (a separable max filter by drawing shifted copies with `lighten`, or `darken` in the Bright look) by about a third of the shrink factor before it is scaled down, so 1-pixel lines remain about a pixel wide; 800 px, 20 frames a second; every frame's delay is the time that really passed; encoding happens in a worker; a frame the palette fits badly gets its own local colour table.
8. **Fallback:** without WebCodecs, MediaRecorder as before, now with the page's sound as a track (`video/mp4;codecs=avc1…,mp4a.40.2` preferred). Such a file may need re-encoding for X (README "Posting on X").
9. **Sound from numbers.** `src/core/ambience.ts` makes a stereo white-noise loop (left and right independent, about −30 dB RMS); white noise has no memory, so it loops without a seam. No recordings, so nothing to license. One `AudioContext` per page, made on the first click or key. (The first 0.4.2 build synthesised crickets and distant traffic; the owner replaced them with white noise the same day.)
10. **Points carry sound** (`PathPoint.sound: 'silence' | 'noise'`; a chart's `ground`), validated and written to `.sp4ce.json` only when used (CLAUDE.md rule 16); the first build's `"field"` is read as `"noise"`. The Starsigns path has the noise bed at every point; it fades in over a second and out when presenting stops.
11. **The ground is an illustration.** `src/fly/field.ts` draws a ground disc 40 km across and ~220 trees (spruce cones and round crowns, three instanced draws) 30–160 m away and 12–30 m tall, all in one flat, unlit colour (an outline: black once the Sun is 6° below the place's horizon, dark green #0f2e17 with it up, blended through twilight), placed by a seed from the place's coordinates, in the place's own east-north-up frame, positioned relative to the camera in double precision. It appears only where a Starsigns chart says `ground`, fades in below 25 km and is full within 2 km; Earth's graticule and line skin hide while it shows (they sit kilometres up). When the point makes Earth see-through (the last pose), **the field falls away from under the viewer**: by equal factors (a metre in half a second, 5 km by two seconds, hundreds by three), fading out over its last second, while the wheel takes the view. This breaks the "never invent surface detail" rule on purpose, at the owner's request, and only for the Starsigns place; real ground comes with the spheres' surface skins (docs/SPHERES.md).
12. **Landing and lifting off** are their own flights in Fly (`land` in `FlyView.tsx`): height above the ground changes by equal factors, and the ground still to cross shrinks in proportion to the height, so you come down on the spot from above. Landing (8 s, the planets' last view to standing in the field) looks down at the spot, then up to the sky; lifting off (6 s) is there for any path that rises from the ground.
13. **A calm final view.** In the see-through pose the wheel sits on a disc in the sky's own colour (50% opaque), its lines at full strength and its planets larger (24, 19, 15 px) with a dark ring and their own glow; behind it the sky is dimmed to under half, only the zodiac's figures (and their names) stay, so the zodiac constellations shine faintly through the wheel, and paths and grids step aside.
14. **One sweep round the sky** (`starsignsPath`, tested on random dates and places): from the Sun's group (seen from pose 1's own camera), the planets' views go east, 2 seconds at each (`SWEEP_PAUSE`), every body once. Turns take the short way, so before this a gap of more than 180° between two views swung the camera back west over sky already done (past the Sun again, before Pluto); a gap over 120° (`MAX_TURN_DEG`) now gets pass-through points with no pause.

## Consequences

- An MP4 from a current Chrome, Edge or Safari uploads to X without re-encoding. Verified in Chromium: ffprobe reports `h264 High yuv420p 1920x1080 30/1 progressive`, `aac LC 48000 stereo`, moov before mdat, and ffmpeg decodes it without an error. AAC encoding itself could not be tested on Linux; the writer was checked with real AAC from ffmpeg.
- Encoding is the browser's (hardware on Macs); on a slow computer the encoder may lag, and the file appears a little after stopping. The Starsigns package receives the MP4 through the same `onBlob` hand-back as before.
- The calendar's JavaScript budget is untouched: the encoder, the writer and the sound synthesis load only when first used.
- If X still refuses a file: re-encode with the FFmpeg line in README "Posting on X", and note X's exact message.
